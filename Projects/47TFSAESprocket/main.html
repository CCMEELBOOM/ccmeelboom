47T FSAE Sprocket

A design study for a 47-tooth FSAE sprocket focused on reducing weight while preserving the requirements needed for reliable drivetrain service.

Overview

Goal

Reduce the sprocket weight by at least 10% while maintaining the functional, packaging, and manufacturing requirements of the FSAE drivetrain.

Design requirements

The sprocket must reduce weight by at least 10% while meeting the drivetrain interface requirements. It must use the existing 47-tooth layout, fit an ISO 606 428 motorcycle chain, and mate with the Drexler V3 160 mm differential used by the car.

Current status

The sprocket and hub have been CAD modeled and evaluated with hand calculations and an ANSYS static structural simulation. The design work is organized around the five-step process below.

Process

Step One / Determine requirements

The first step was defining the interfaces and performance target for the sprocket:

  • Reduce sprocket weight by at least 10%.
  • Use the required 47-tooth geometry.
  • Fit an ISO 606 428 motorcycle chain.
  • Fit the Drexler V3 160 mm differential hub.

Interface constraints

The chain standard controls the tooth profile and pitch, while the differential determines the hub geometry, mounting pattern, and packaging constraints. Both interfaces had to be established before material could be removed from the sprocket.

Step Two / Determine loading and restraints

The primary load case starts with 107 N·m of torque from the motor. That torque was carried through the chain back-tension equation to determine the force applied at the teeth of the 47-tooth sprocket. The restraint condition represents the sprocket and hub being supported through their mounting interface to the Drexler differential.

Load path

The resulting tooth force is transferred through the sprocket web and hub into the differential mounting interface. Defining this load path established the inputs for both the hand calculations and the ANSYS static simulation.

Step Three / Hand calculations

Hand calculations were used to convert motor torque into chain tension and tooth loading before running finite-element analysis. These calculations provided a first check on the expected stresses and helped verify that the simulation setup was using a reasonable force.

Calculation basis

The calculation workbook documents the tooth-force calculation and served as an independent engineering check against the later ANSYS results rather than relying on the simulation alone.

Step Four / CAD the sprocket and hub

The sprocket and its hub were modeled in CAD around the 428 chain interface and Drexler differential mounting requirements. The geometry was developed so that weight-reduction features could be evaluated without changing the required chain or differential interfaces.

CAD focus

The CAD stage connected the tooth profile, sprocket web, and differential hub into one assembly-ready design for structural evaluation.

Step Five / ANSYS static simulation

The final step was setting up an ANSYS static structural simulation using the calculated tooth loading and hub restraints. The simulation was used to evaluate how the sprocket responds to the motor-torque load case and to check the lightweight design against the hand calculations.

Validation

The design was assessed by comparing the finite-element response with the analytical load path and the original weight-reduction target. The simulation report is available in the project resources below.

Design and FEA Comparison

Old versus revised sprocket

The revised sprocket changes the web geometry and integrates the hub interface for the Drexler differential. The side-by-side FEA results show how each design responds to its documented static structural setup. The minimum factor of safety is below 1.0 in both results and is concentrated at the mounting bores, but the revised design shows higher factors of safety throughout much of the rest of the part. The previous year's sprocket operated without failure during vehicle use, and the revised design has a similar overall factor-of-safety response, supporting use of the simulated geometry as a design basis while the bore regions remain areas for attention.

Factor of safety / old design

Reported minimum factor of safety: 0.36473.

Factor of safety / revised design

Reported minimum factor of safety: 0.5503 at a mounting-bore region. The revised design has higher safety factors across much of the remaining sprocket compared with the old design.
