47T FSAE Sprocket

Overview

Goal and requirements

Reduce weight by at least 10% while maintaining the 47-tooth layout, ISO 606 428 chain fit, Drexler V3 160 mm differential interface, four mounting bores, and manufacturing constraints. The final redesign achieved a 57% reduction.

Current status

The sprocket and hub were CAD modeled, hand-checked, and evaluated with ANSYS static structural simulation. The drawing has been submitted for a quote; the hub is planned for in-house machining on a 3-axis Haas CNC mill.

Optimization Theory

From motor torque to tooth loading

The motor produces 107 N·m and the 10-tooth motor sprocket has a 41 mm pitch-circle diameter.

\[r_m=\frac{41}{2}=20.5\text{ mm}=0.0205\text{ m}\]
\[F_t=\frac{T_m}{r_m}=\frac{107}{0.0205}\approx5.22\text{ kN}\]

The moving chain adds centrifugal tension:

\[F_c=\lambda v^2\qquad\Rightarrowqquad F_{chain}=F_t+F_c\approx5{,}357\text{ N}\]

The total tension is distributed using the back-tension calculation. The first tooth carries the greatest load, and the first 11 teeth carry approximately 99% of the total. Those tooth forces become the sprocket and hub load inputs.

Hub load model and optimization

At each hub radius, the tooth load creates an acting moment:

\[M_{acting}=F_{chain}r\qquad F(r)=\frac{M_{acting}}{r}\]

For the 11 loaded teeth, the loaded arc is approximated as (11/47) of the circumference:

\[A=\left(\frac{11}{47}\right)2\pi rt\qquad\tau(r)=\frac{M_{acting}}{\left(\frac{11}{47}\right)2\pi r^2t}\]

Stress falls with (r^2), so material farther from the center can be removed while retaining the load path around the hub and bolts.

Checks and hub analysis

The design target was a factor of safety of at least 3. The required checks were:

  • Shear stress at every radius (r) on the hub.
  • Bearing yield at the mounting-bolt locations.
  • Spline shear and compression.
\[n_d=\frac{S_{ys}}{\tau}\qquad\sigma_{bearing}=\frac{F}{Dt}\]

The spline was not analyzed separately because it is supplied directly by Drexler. The M10 bearing iteration produced a documented factor of safety of 4.245 and approximately 9.23 mm local thickness.

Process

Step One / Determine requirements

The requirements were a minimum 10% reduction, 47 teeth, ISO 606 428 chain compatibility, the Drexler differential interface, and four mounting bores.

Mounting position

This CAD view established the packaging relationship between the sprocket, differential, chain, and drivetrain.

Step Two / Determine loading and restraints

The worst case was differential lockup. The spline was fixed, tooth loads came from back tension, and reactions travelled through the web, bolts, hub, and differential.

Loading equations

\[F_t=\frac{107}{0.0205}\approx5.22\text{ kN}\]
\[F_{chain}=F_t+\lambda v^2\approx5{,}357\text{ N}\]
\[F_{tooth,1}>F_{tooth,2}>\cdots>F_{tooth,11}\]

Step Three / Hand calculations

The calculations converted chain tension into hub moment and checked shear stress at each radius.

\[M_{acting}=F_{chain}r\]
\[\tau(r)=\frac{M_{acting}}{\left(\frac{11}{47}\right)2\pi r^2t}\]

Thickness and bearing

\[t=\frac{6M_{acting}}{\left(\frac{11}{47}\right)2\pi r^2S_{yt}}\]
\[\sigma_{bearing}=\frac{F}{Dt}\qquad n_d\geq3\]

These checks set the 7.31 mm web thickness and thicker material around the bores.

Step Four / CAD the sprocket and hub

The CAD model combined the chain profile, clearance, mounting pattern, spline, differential-fit region, and lightweight web.

Geometry development

Cutouts removed low-value material while preserving a continuous load path and improving heat dissipation.

Step Five / ANSYS static simulation

The simulation used the calculated tooth loads, 75% bolt-strength preload, and a fixed spline representing the differential interface.

Simulation response

The revised result was compared directly with last year's sprocket under the corresponding static setup.

Interpretation: The displayed minimum factor of safety remains below 1.0 and does not represent a final pass condition. It is concentrated at the bores. Last year's sprocket operated without failure, and this year's overall response is similar while showing higher factors of safety through much of the part, so the simulated geometry is a useful design basis with the bore regions still requiring attention.

Step Six / Quoting

The manufacturer drawing was submitted for a quote. It defines the 47-tooth profile, 428 chain geometry, four-bolt pattern, hub dimensions, and 7.31 mm section thickness. The hub is planned for machining on a 3-axis Haas CNC mill.
