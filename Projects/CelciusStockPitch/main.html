Celcius Stock Pitch

From January to April 2025, my team competed in the Financial Markets Lab Stock Pitch Competition and pitched Celsius Holdings. My role focused on valuation, comparable-company analysis, and setting the final price target using SEC filings, Bloomberg Terminal research, and Excel-based valuation work.

Overview

Problem

The competition required each team to select one of the provided companies, perform diligence, and present a full investment pitch. Our team chose Celsius Holdings because the company had strong growth potential, but its valuation depended heavily on how it compared to larger public beverage companies.

My challenge was to determine a reasonable price target. That meant identifying true public comparables, analyzing revenue and earnings trends, and deciding whether a basic EV/Revenue or EV/Earnings approach fairly represented Celsius’s growth profile.

Solution

I started with Celsius’s 10-K report from SEC.gov and pulled current and historical revenue and earnings data from 2020 to 2025 into Excel. I then used the school’s Bloomberg Terminal to research public comparable companies and reviewed names such as PepsiCo, Coca-Cola, Campbell’s, and Monster Beverage.

After reviewing each company’s exposure to the energy drink market, I concluded that Monster was the strongest public comparable. Other companies owned or distributed energy drink brands, but their overall revenue mix was too broad for their valuation multiples to accurately represent a pure energy drink business.

Process

Starting With the 10-K

My first step was to go through Celsius’s 10-K report and extract the business overview, revenue, and earnings data needed for the valuation. I copied the values from 2020 through 2025 into an Excel spreadsheet where I built the comparable-company analysis.

This gave me a foundation for the pitch because I could compare Celsius’s growth and profitability against other beverage companies instead of relying only on narrative research.

Finding the Right Comparable

The Bloomberg Terminal provided a starting list of public comparables, including companies like PepsiCo, Coca-Cola, Campbell’s, and Monster. At first, this looked like a useful peer set, but the more I researched each company, the more it became clear that most were not true energy drink comparables.

PepsiCo and Coca-Cola both had connections to energy drink brands, but their energy drink exposure was only a small part of their broader business. Because of that, their overall valuation multiples were not truly driven by energy drink performance. Monster Beverage was the only comparable that closely matched Celsius’s category exposure.

Comparable-company research used to narrow the peer set.

Building the Valuation

Once Monster was selected as the main comparable, I reviewed Monster’s 10-K reports and pulled revenue and earnings data for the same historical period. From there, I calculated EV/Revenue multiples across the years and used Monster’s current valuation multiple to estimate a Celsius price target.

The initial EV/Revenue valuation produced a 2025 price target of $55.17. One improvement I identified afterward would have been to analyze Monster’s multiples over a longer ten-year period, then use that trend to better estimate a future multiple for Celsius.

Adjusting for Growth

I was also curious about the difference between EV/Revenue and EV/Earnings valuation approaches. Monster’s raw EV/Revenue multiple reflected a more mature, slower-growing company, so applying it directly to Celsius risked undervaluing the company.

To account for that, the adjusted EV/Revenue column scaled Monster’s multiple upward to better reflect Celsius’s higher growth profile. The correction factor itself was not displayed on the slide, but the adjustment is captured in the difference between the raw Monster EV/Revenue multiple and the adjusted EV/Revenue multiple.

EV/Revenue correction used to better reflect Celsius’s growth profile.

Final Pitch and Takeaways

The final pitch combined research, valuation, business analysis, and presentation work across the full team. My contribution centered on explaining why Monster was the most useful public comparable, how the price target was developed, and how the adjusted EV/Revenue method better reflected Celsius’s growth stage.

This project taught me how much judgment goes into valuation. The math matters, but so does the reasoning behind which companies should actually be compared. The team placed 1st in the competition, and the price target was later met within approximately three months.
